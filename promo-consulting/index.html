01

The shape of the partnership

You asked: carve out the Facilis membership for PC. Anything a Syncore distributor wants automated, not only finance, comes through Promo Consulting, so PC is never competing against AltOps or a third party in its own space.

Revised We understand the ask, and we understand why: if your clients see AltOps inside the product and the next distributor goes to us directly, your marketing has become our marketing. We have a different answer to that problem than a category exclusive, and we think it gets you the same protection.

Territory follows the route to the client, not the software they run.

  • Through PC, it is PC's. Any Facilis distributor that PC introduces, sells to or onboards is PC's client for the full scope: bookkeeping, CSR, stores, order entry, quoting, sales support, all of it. AltOps will not sell to that client directly, or through another partner, during the term and for a period after it.
  • Direct to AltOps, it is AltOps'. A Facilis distributor that reaches us on its own, or through one of our other channels, is ours to serve at our own pricing. We will not route them through PC, and we will not ask PC to give us the ones you find.
  • Existing AltOps clients stay where they are. The handful we already run production work for, including the ones you know, are grandfathered as you said on the call. If one of them later asks to buy the PC suite, that is their decision and yours.

The reason this works without confusing the market is the next answer: nobody using PC's product will see AltOps in it, so the two offerings do not look related from the outside. PC sells a Promo Consulting ecosystem for Syncore distributors. AltOps sells AltOps. A distributor who hears about both is hearing about two different companies, and the one with the twenty years of Syncore relationships is you.

We are not able to agree that every Syncore distributor in the industry must come through PC, because Facilis is also where our own go-to-market has been pointed and we have relationships there that predate this conversation. We would rather say that plainly than sign something we would spend the term working around.

You asked: white-label versus co-branded. On the call you said "PC AI, powered by AltOps" was tolerable only with a protected market, and you would rather not have AltOps visible at all.

Revised Fully white-labelled. Since we are not asking you for a category exclusive, we no longer need visibility in your product to justify one.

  • Promo Consulting's name and logo only. No AltOps mark, no "powered by", no AltOps in the footer.
  • Your domain for sign-in, your name on system emails and notifications, your name in the audit trail your clients see.
  • Support goes out under PC. Where our engineers are on a client call, they are there as your technical team.
  • The partner admin tier is built white-labelled from the start, not re-skinned afterwards.

What we keep is the reference value between the two of us: we can name Promo Consulting as a partner in our own conversations, with your approval on wording, and you can name AltOps as your platform where a client's due diligence requires it.

You said: agents are becoming a commodity. What distributors need is a new way of running a department, a bookkeeping bundle or a CSR bundle, not one-off agents based on whatever pain point a staff member raises.

Answered Agreed, and we will build to that. Role bundles rather than agent counts, a version one that gets a department running and a version two that extends it, and PC deciding what a client needs rather than asking. The platform does not care how many agents sit inside a bundle, so the packaging is entirely yours.

You asked: should AltOps just license through Facilis directly instead?

Answered We heard your view on the call and it matched what we had seen: the institutional knowledge left with the people, three of whom are at PC, and an affiliate listing does not carry trust when the subject is a distributor's books. That is why we would rather build the Syncore back office with PC than through a program listing. It does not change the territory answer above, but it is why we are here.

What the floor does

Every client costs us real money whether they pay PC $2,000 or $10,000: their own cloud machine, model usage on every run, a verification cycle on every change, and an engineer on call when Syncore changes a screen. The floor makes each client sustainable on its own. It stops binding once a client pays PC more than $5,000 a month, and above that the share is a flat 30%.

At thirty to fifty clients in year one that is a meaningful business for both of us, and the share improves for PC as bundles are added to each client, because the floor falls away.

You asked: what is AI worth to a distributor, and how should PC price the bundles? You do not want to be three times our public price, and you do not want us setting a market perception that undercuts you.

Answered Here is what we know and what we will do about it.

  • Our direct pricing today. An entry plan at $1,500 a month covering up to five agents, and $3,000 a month for clients with heavier or more complex workflows. That is the price of the software with our own onboarding and support, sold to distributors who come to us.
  • What PC's bundle carries that ours does not. The department design, the best-practice guardrails, the benchmark against other distributors, the audit and reconciliation work around it, and the person who tells the owner what they need. A bookkeeper costs $24,000 a year offshore and $75,000 onshore, and a bundle that replaces most of that work is priced against the role, not against our software.
  • No surprises on price. We will share our price list with you and tell you before any change to it, so PC always prices with full knowledge of what a distributor could get from us directly.

The concrete next step, which we agreed on the call: you record a walkthrough of what Syncore Bookkeeping version one should do, we replicate it on the platform in about a day, and within a week we give you the exact cost of running it. Then you price the bundle with your margin on top, and we both know the number is real.

You asked: how do we stop runaway usage disrupting the pricing model, and explain scale-to-zero in plain English.

Answered Unchanged from 9/6, and worth keeping in view because it is what makes the floor work. Model cost is ours, not PC's and not the client's, so runaway usage is our margin at risk. We meter per client and per run and can cap any client that behaves unusually. The first runs of a new workflow are reasoned by the model and cost real money; once the path is recorded, replay costs a fraction of that. Scale-to-zero switches a client's machine off when nobody is working and back on when a task arrives, with logins intact, so infrastructure cost tracks usage rather than client count.

Who owns what

You asked: we agree on who owns what. Can it be defined explicitly in the agreement, including the breakup process? And what did you mean by pressure-testing the IP line?

Answered Yes, and nothing more is needed than plain English in the contract. The line, as we said it on the call and as you agreed:

On exit, PC exports the methodology, the rules with their reasoning, the workflow specifications and the execution history, and we delete our copy with written confirmation. No future AltOps service will use what we learned from PC's knowledge. You said on the call that you do not want to own the technology, and we do not want to own your knowledge, so the line is clean.

You asked: what development is needed to make client knowledge, not data, available to PC so you can improve processes and agents with the client's permission? You want to analyse SOPs against your benchmarks automatically, protect owners from staff redesigning processes badly, and escalate to the owner when a requested change makes no sense.

Answered The core of it exists today. When we build an automation from a recording, the platform generates a structured SOP of how that client does the work: the steps, the rules, the exceptions, with none of the underlying data. That SOP is available to the client and, with their consent, to PC.

What we would build for PC on top of it, as part of the partner tier:

  • A methodology library holding PC's benchmarks and best-practice SOPs as the starting point, owned and edited by PC.
  • Change review. When a client requests a process change, the request is compared against PC's benchmark and against how other PC clients do the same work, and PC gets a recommendation with the reasoning. PC decides; nothing is automatic.
  • Owner escalation. Where PC recommends against a change, the client acknowledges the recommendation with a click and the owner is notified before it goes ahead. That is the layer of protection you described, and it is a strong thing to sell.
  • Case studies. Recurring requests and PC's standard replies accumulate in the library, so the answer to "we want to change invoicing like this" is drafted from what PC has already decided.

Consent lives in PC's client agreement, as you said the Facilis community is used to. Client-identifying content is stripped when an SOP is promoted into the library, and no client's data ever moves. We are happy to help design the analysis sequence. The benchmarks and the outputs are PC's knowledge.

05

The platform

You asked: logins. PC does not want human intervention on every run, and you want a separate login per agent in the client's system so the audit trail is clean.

Answered The client logs in to Syncore, QuickBooks, their bank and the rest inside the AltOps browser themselves. Credentials never reach us and we do not see them. Sessions persist, so nobody re-authenticates per run; about once every thirty days one person logs back in, and that is the only touch.

For the audit trail we recommend each client creates a dedicated login for the agent, the same way your staff each have their own login in a client's system. One agent login is enough: every agent running under it is identified individually in the platform's own record, so you can show exactly which agent did what, and the client's staff cannot say Promo Consulting must have done it.

You asked: if the system goes down, the client cannot simply go back to doing it manually because they no longer have the staff. What recovery commitment can we put in writing?

For the call Two layers, and then a number we should agree together.

  • Model failover is built in. Anthropic is the primary model. If it errors, runs fall back to OpenAI or Google automatically. All three are under commercial terms that prohibit training on your clients' data. A single vendor's outage does not stop a run.
  • Work is queued, not lost. An outage delays; it does not destroy. When service resumes the queue drains at several times human speed.

On the call you drew the useful distinction: forty-eight hours is survivable for month-end finance, but purchase orders and CSR work cannot wait that long. We would write recovery targets into the agreement by workflow type rather than one blanket number, and we would rather set those with you than pick them here.

You asked: Trillium is calling on the same clients. What is the difference?

Answered Without speaking for them, the difference we see is in approach.

  • API-first versus API plus browser. An API-only tool can do what the vendor exposed. Our agents use the API where one exists and drive the screen where it does not, so the work that lives in spreadsheets, PowerPoints, artwork and email is inside scope rather than outside it. That is the moat, and it is why the Syncore work that has no endpoint is possible.
  • Reasoning that becomes replay. The first few runs of a workflow are reasoned by the model and cost real money. The platform records the path and later runs replay it directly, so a run that cost $10 the first time costs cents by the fourth. Cheaper and more consistent, which matters for books.
  • Both are funded. They by Y Combinator, we by Mucker Capital. Neither of us is going away in the next twelve months, and we would rather you compare the platforms than the balance sheets.

A recent example: a distributor on shopVOX with overseas factory sheets moved from Trillium to us because their quoting depended on reading product images and matching them to codes, which is browser work. We can arrange a reference if useful.

You asked: does an agent behave like an employee, on a schedule, reporting to a human, with its own audit trail? And can it work faster than human pace?

Answered Unchanged from 9/6. An agent has its own identity, its own isolated machine and its own login, runs on a schedule, reports to a named person and escalates what is not its to decide. Volume is a matter of how many run in parallel, not how fast one types, and non-urgent work runs overnight.

06

Security and compliance

You asked: a real range for SOC 2, and whether Type I lets us say "SOC 2 compliant".

Answered Type I in roughly six to eight weeks, on Thoropass's current expectation for us. Type II within a month to six weeks after that, which puts full SOC 2 Type II before the end of the year. We are a small codebase with few people and devices to audit, which is why the timeline is short compared with a larger vendor's.

On wording: once Type I is issued we will say SOC 2 Type I, and the badge says so. We will not describe Type I as Type II, and we would ask PC to hold the same line with clients. The moment Thoropass fixes the dates we send them to you directly.

You asked: insurance. PC carries a $5 million liability policy and a separate cyber policy because you are inside your clients' books. What does AltOps carry, what limit is worthwhile, and can PC be a named insured on our policy?

For the call We owe you the specifics and will bring them: our current cover, and a proposed limit sized against what your largest prospects' contracts require. In principle, yes to PC as a named insured on our cyber and technology errors and omissions cover, subject to what the policy allows, and we will confirm that with our broker. PC carrying its own cover as well is the right belt and braces. This should be drafted alongside tight end-client contract language.

You asked: data versus knowledge versus training, in practical terms.

Answered Unchanged. Client data is the working context of a single run and is gone when the run ends; it is not retained to train anything, by us or by the model providers, and it never reaches another client. Knowledge is a written rule stored as a record you can read, edit and export. Model training means changing a model's weights, which we do not do and our providers do not do with commercial API traffic. Knowledge only moves if a person decides to move it, under the consent described in section 3.

You asked: US-only data residency, deletion on exit with written confirmation, and a security document for prospects and RFPs.

Answered US residency becomes a contractual commitment, since it is already true. Deletion is a logged operation with written confirmation, and an audited control once SOC 2 is in place. We will produce a buyer-facing security overview that PC can hand to prospects under PC's name, and we will answer client questionnaires on your behalf during an RFP.
